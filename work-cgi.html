CGI Inc. Bell Canada

Business Analyst → Associate PM on a GenAI pilot

Turned scattered stakeholder ideas into prioritised GenAI use cases — and delivered customer enquiry automation in about four months while helping the team move waterfall → agile.

  • Problem
  • Approach
  • Role
  • Outcome
  • Learnings

Problem

Telecom stakeholders had many GenAI ideas but little shared structure — no clear prioritisation, success criteria, or delivery rhythm.

The wider delivery culture was still heavily waterfall, which made mid-cycle change expensive and abstract “agile training” ineffective.

Approach

  • As BSA: documented workflows, system design, and process maps so GenAI work sat on a clear operational baseline.
  • As APM: structured scattered ideas into prioritised use cases with explicit success criteria.
  • Drove the GenAI pilot toward a concrete first outcome — customer enquiry automation — instead of an open-ended platform wish list.
  • Used a real mid-cycle requirement-change example (not slideware) to help the team feel why agile practices matter — and stayed honest about agile’s limits.
  • During broader restructuring, kept focus on milestones and transparent communication.

Role

Business System Analyst → Associate Product Manager (GenAI pilot) on Bell Canada work at CGI.

Outcome

  • Customer enquiry automation delivered in roughly 4 months.
  • Use cases and success metrics gave the pilot a shared decision frame.
  • Team shifted practice toward agile using lived delivery pain, not only training decks.

Learnings

  • GenAI pilots fail when “ideas” stay unranked — success criteria are the product.
  • Process change sticks when you teach from a concrete failure the team already felt.
  • In restructuring, milestone clarity and honesty beat polished status theatre.
